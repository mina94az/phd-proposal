\chapter{Future Work: Predicting Numerical Variability}
\label{chap:future-work}

\section{Motivation and objective}

The first phase of this research demonstrated that numerical variability (NV) in functional MRI graph measures is measurable, spatially heterogeneous, and dependent on the graph measure, network threshold, and preprocessing strategy. It also showed that numerical perturbations can influence statistical conclusions when an observed effect is close to the significance threshold. These findings establish the importance of evaluating numerical stability, but routine evaluation remains difficult. Monte Carlo Arithmetic (MCA) requires several perturbed executions of the processing workflow; in Phase~I, ten MCA executions required an aggregate processing time approximately 12.9 times that of one standard execution. Moreover, most researchers do not routinely have access to an MCA-instrumented workflow or the resources required to repeat preprocessing for every participant.

The next phase will therefore investigate whether the numerical sensitivity of graph measures can be estimated from a single standard processing run. In this chapter, \emph{IEEE functional connectivity (FC)} refers to the connectivity matrix obtained from an ordinary execution using standard IEEE floating-point arithmetic, without MCA instrumentation. The central premise is that the IEEE connectome may already contain information about its susceptibility to numerical perturbations. For example, correlations close to a graph-construction threshold may cross that threshold following a small perturbation, changing the graph topology and, consequently, its derived measures. If this information can be identified and used predictively, numerical stability could be screened at substantially lower computational cost.

The objective is not to replace MCA in all settings. Repeated perturbation remains necessary for constructing reference outcomes, validating predictions, and evaluating workflows outside the scope of a trained model. Rather, the proposed work aims to determine when an IEEE-only estimate can identify participants or graph measures that are likely to be numerically sensitive and should receive more extensive stability analysis.

\section{Preliminary investigation of variability propagation}
\label{sec:future-preliminary}

An exploratory analysis was conducted to address the question: \emph{How do MCA perturbations in functional connectivity propagate into graph-metric numerical variability?} The analysis used matched IEEE connectomes and ten MCA realizations from 154 participants with Parkinson's disease. It examined binary and weighted graphs over several correlation thresholds and included controlled recomputations in which either graph topology or connection weights were held fixed. This analysis was explanatory rather than predictive: quantities observed across MCA realizations were used to investigate the propagation mechanism, whereas a deployable predictor must rely only on information available from the standard IEEE execution.

The preliminary findings support a coherent mechanism. First, MCA perturbations did not affect all functional connections uniformly. Second, connections whose IEEE correlation values were close to the graph threshold were more likely to change their inclusion status across MCA realizations. This threshold-crossing behaviour amplified small FC perturbations into changes in graph topology. For binary graphs, holding the IEEE topology fixed eliminated NV, whereas allowing MCA-derived edge inclusion changes reproduced the variability of the full MCA graphs. Thus, within the evaluated setting, binary graph NV was attributable to topological changes. Weighted graph measures were influenced by both changes in connection weights and changes in topology.

IEEE-derived features were also strongly associated with graph-measure NV in several settings. In particular, the proportion of connections near the threshold was associated with the NV of local graph measures at low and intermediate thresholds. These associations became less consistent in sparse graphs, indicating that the number of vulnerable connections alone may not be sufficient: their locations and topological roles may also be important. The results therefore provide a mechanistic basis for considering FC magnitude, distance to the graph threshold, density, and IEEE graph organization as candidate predictors. Because these analyses were exploratory and were conducted before the final quality-control decisions, they will be reassessed in the finalized cohort.

\section{Preliminary feasibility of IEEE-only prediction}

Initial prediction experiments evaluated whether graph-measure NV could be estimated using only the standard IEEE connectome. A random forest was applied to vectorized FC features, and a compact convolutional neural network was applied to the connectivity matrix. The neural-network experiments used a logarithmic transformation of the NV target to reduce its strong positive skew. Models were evaluated on participants not used for model fitting.

The preliminary results indicate that prediction is feasible for some graph measures and thresholds, but not uniformly across all outcomes. At lower thresholds, degree and eigenvector-centrality NV were among the more promising targets. At intermediate and higher thresholds, betweenness and clustering NV showed clearer predictive signal, while path length and small-worldness were promising in some of the sparser-graph configurations. Other combinations, including several global-efficiency and degree outcomes, showed limited or inconsistent predictive performance. The random forest frequently matched or exceeded the compact convolutional model, suggesting that model complexity alone does not improve prediction in the current sample.

Performance also changed with quality-control exclusions and the participant-splitting strategy. This sensitivity is scientifically important because image quality, failed processing runs, and the precision of the MCA-derived target may affect apparent predictability. Consequently, these experiments are treated as preliminary evidence of feasibility rather than final performance estimates. They motivate a more controlled comparison in which the cohort, quality-control criteria, target construction, and validation procedure are fixed before model evaluation.

\section{Research questions}

The proposed phase will address the following questions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Which properties of a standard IEEE connectome---including FC magnitude, distance to the graph threshold, network density, and graph organization---are associated with susceptibility to numerical perturbations?
    \item Can participant-level NV in graph measures be predicted out of sample from a single IEEE execution, and for which graph measures and thresholds is prediction sufficiently accurate to be useful?
    \item Do FC-derived features provide information beyond conventional image quality-control metrics, and how robust are the predictions to quality-control decisions, preprocessing choices, and participant composition?
    \item Do simple, interpretable models perform comparably to more complex models, and can the resulting predictions generalize beyond the configuration in which they were trained?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Proposed research plan}

The study will first establish a finalized reference dataset of matched IEEE and MCA outputs. Cohort selection, image quality-control rules, the handling of failed MCA executions, and the minimum number of valid realizations will be specified before confirmatory model evaluation. For each participant, graph measure, and threshold, the reference NV target will be calculated from repeated MCA realizations. Targets will initially summarize participant-level variability; regional predictions may be considered after the feasibility of participant-level prediction has been established.

Candidate predictors will be restricted to information available without repeated perturbation. They will include conventional quality-control measures, interpretable summaries of the IEEE connectome, and the full IEEE FC representation. The interpretable features will be guided by the preliminary propagation analysis and will include summaries of threshold proximity, network density, FC magnitude, and IEEE graph measures. Comparing QC-only, FC-summary, and full-connectome models will determine whether the connectome provides predictive information beyond general data quality and whether the full high-dimensional representation adds value beyond mechanistically motivated summaries.

The primary model comparison will prioritize regularized regression and tree-based methods because they are appropriate for the current sample size and permit clearer interpretation of predictive features. A compact neural-network model will be retained as a benchmark for potential nonlinear spatial structure in the connectivity matrix. Target transformations, including the logarithmic transformation used in the preliminary experiments, will be estimated within the training data and evaluated consistently across models.

All validation will be performed at the participant level so that no information from a held-out participant contributes to preprocessing, feature selection, model tuning, or target transformation. Models will be compared on identical participant splits using held-out coefficient of determination, absolute prediction error, and rank correlation. Reporting both magnitude-based and rank-based measures is necessary because a model may correctly rank participants by numerical sensitivity while still providing inaccurate estimates of its magnitude. Sensitivity analyses will evaluate the effect of graph threshold, QC definition, confound-regression strategy, and the number of MCA realizations used to construct the reference target. If suitable data are available, the final stage will assess transfer to an additional acquisition, participant group, or processing configuration.

\section{Expected contribution and scope}

This phase is expected to provide two related contributions. First, it will clarify how small perturbations in FC are converted into variability in network topology and graph measures. Second, it will establish whether numerical fragility can be estimated from a standard connectome at a useful level of accuracy. A successful predictor could serve as a screening tool: participants or outcomes assigned high predicted NV could be prioritized for repeated perturbation-based analysis, while consistently stable settings could require fewer computational resources.

The scope and final implementation of this phase remain under development and may change as the quality-control analysis and confirmatory experiments progress. Predictive performance is expected to differ across graph measures and construction thresholds, and success for one processing configuration will not imply generalization to other datasets or pipelines. The intended outcome is therefore a validated, explicitly bounded procedure together with evidence identifying the situations in which direct MCA assessment remains necessary.
