\chapter{Predicting Numerical Variability from Standard Functional Connectivity}
\label{chap:future-work}

\section*{Abstract}

Numerical perturbations introduced during fMRI preprocessing can propagate to functional connectivity (FC) and its derived graph measures. Assessing this numerical variability (NV) is important because it may affect statistical conclusions, particularly when observed effects are close to decision thresholds, potentially contributing to false-positive or false-negative findings. However, directly quantifying NV using Monte Carlo Arithmetic (MCA) requires repeated preprocessing runs and may be impractical for routine studies.

This phase investigates whether NV can be predicted from the FC matrix produced by a single standard IEEE execution. The current feasibility analysis focuses on 146 participants with Parkinson's disease and uses retained MCA repetitions only to define participant-level NV targets. Two complementary initial models are considered: M0, a BrainNetCNN model that learns directly from the complete signed FC matrix, and Ridge regression using a compact vector of nine IEEE-derived features. These features summarize threshold proximity, graph density, and the structural consequences of potential edge changes. Across the six reported graph measures and three thresholds, the updated Ridge model produced higher held-out $R^2$ than M0 for 17 of 18 endpoints and higher Spearman correlation for 16. Because the explicit validity-fraction feature was removed after inspection of an earlier held-out failure, these findings remain exploratory and require confirmation in untouched data.

\section{Introduction}

Phase~I demonstrated that NV in fMRI graph measures is measurable and can influence statistical conclusions. Although repeated MCA executions provide an empirical estimate of this uncertainty, their computational cost and specialized instrumentation limit routine use. The main research question of the present phase is therefore:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Can the numerical variability of graph measures be predicted from the functional connectome produced by a single standard IEEE execution?}
\end{quote}

This question is plausible because MCA perturbations do not affect FC uniformly. Connections close to a graph threshold are particularly susceptible to changing their inclusion status, and the consequence depends on the network location of the affected connection. Thus, a standard IEEE connectome may contain information about numerical sensitivity through its complete FC organization, threshold proximity, and the structural consequences of potential edge changes.

The present work evaluates two initial representations of this information. M0 asks whether a connectome-oriented neural network can learn NV directly from the complete FC matrix. Ridge regression asks whether a compact nine-feature representation is sufficient for prediction. These models establish preliminary baselines from which a more robust prediction or high-sensitivity screening method can be developed.

\section{Data and methodology}

\subsection{Data, quality control, and prediction targets}

The analysis reused the PPMI data and processing outputs described in Chapter~\ref{chap:phase-one} and was restricted to the Parkinson's disease population. A deliberately minimal quality-control protocol excluded only clear acquisition or processing failures. Each resting-state acquisition was expected to contain 240 volumes; two participants with severely incomplete series of 92 and 13 volumes were excluded. Participants were also excluded when spatial registration failed or visual inspection revealed gross image-integrity defects, including large signal voids or pronounced spatial discontinuities. Within retained acquisitions, volumes flagged as non-steady-state were excluded before FC estimation.
After quality control and matching to the current prediction cohort, 146 participants had a valid IEEE connectome and retained MCA outputs. Of these, 140 had ten retained MCA repetitions, five had nine, and one had eight.

As an initial feasibility experiment, the models were evaluated at three of the six absolute-correlation thresholds examined in Chapter~\ref{chap:phase-one}: 0.05, 0.20, and 0.50. We report the same six binary graph measures: degree centrality, betweenness centrality, clustering coefficient, and eigenvector centrality as local measures, and average shortest path length and small-worldness as global measures. The prediction target was the MCA-derived numerical variability of each graph measure. For a local measure, its standard deviation across retained MCA repetitions was calculated separately at each of the 100 regions and then averaged across regions. For a global measure, the target was its standard deviation across repetitions. All reported targets were available for 146 participants except average shortest path length and small-worldness at threshold 0.50, for which 116 participants had finite targets. The preliminary models therefore attempt to predict participant-level graph-measure variability from a single IEEE connectome, without requiring MCA repetitions at prediction time.

\subsection{Initial prediction models}

\paragraph{M0: complete FC matrix.}
M0 provides the full-connectome baseline. It receives the complete symmetric, zero-diagonal IEEE FC matrix as a $1\times100\times100$ tensor, retaining the signed correlations without thresholding or engineered inputs. A BrainNetCNN encoder uses edge-to-edge and edge-to-node operations to preserve connectome organization \cite{Kawahara2017BrainNetCNN}, followed by a dense layer, LeakyReLU activations, dropout, and a shared output layer. The saved implementation was trained jointly on nine graph-measure targets, although only the six outcomes specified above are reported here. If $f_{\theta}(C_s)$ is the representation learned from participant $s$'s FC matrix, the prediction for measure $g$ is
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{z}_{s,g}^{\mathrm{M0}}
    =\mathbf{w}_{g}^{\mathsf T}f_{\theta}(C_s)+b_g,
    \label{eq:m0-prediction}
\end{equation}
where $z_{s,g}$ is the training-standardized NV target, $\theta$ contains the shared encoder parameters, and $\mathbf{w}_g$ and $b_g$ are measure-specific output parameters. Four prespecified configurations varied the edge-to-edge channels (4 or 8), edge-to-node channels (8 or 16), hidden width (16 or 32), dropout (0.2--0.4), learning rate, and weight decay. Configuration and epoch were selected by inner validation. Training used AdamW, a masked SmoothL1 loss, and two outer-training refits whose inverse-scaled, nonnegative predictions were averaged. M0 is an initial benchmark rather than an optimized final architecture.

\paragraph{M1 auxiliary features: threshold proximity and structural consequences of potential edge changes.}
For each graph measure and threshold, Ridge regression receives a compact vector of nine features derived from the single IEEE connectome. Let $M=4{,}950$ be the number of possible undirected edges and let $d_e=\left|\,|C_e|-\tau\right|$ be edge $e$'s distance from threshold $\tau$. Candidate edges satisfy $d_e\leq0.02$, and at most the 200 closest are evaluated. If toggling candidate edge $e$ changes a local graph measure at region $i$ by $\delta_{e,i}$, its mean absolute and root-mean-square regional effects are $a_e=\operatorname{mean}_i|\delta_{e,i}|$ and $r_e=\sqrt{\operatorname{mean}_i\delta_{e,i}^2}$. For a global measure, $a_e=r_e=|\delta_e|$. Let $\mathcal{F}$ denote the candidate edges with finite responses and $K_{\mathrm{valid}}=|\mathcal{F}|$.

These nine variables were retained from the original compact representation without changing their definitions. The explicit finite-influence-fraction predictor and its missing-value indicator were removed in a follow-up analysis prompted by an extrapolation failure in the earlier ten-feature model. The quantities $K_{\mathrm{valid}}$ and baseline availability remain part of the influence calculations but are not supplied as separate predictors.

\paragraph{Compact Ridge regression.}
A separate Ridge model is fitted for every measure and threshold using the nine features above. Each numeric feature is accompanied by a binary missing-value indicator, giving a fixed 18-slot processed representation. Let $\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{s,g}^{(\tau)}\in\mathbb{R}^{18}$ denote this representation and $z_{s,g}^{(\tau)}$ the training-standardized NV target. The model is
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{z}_{s,g}^{(\tau)}
    =b_g+\boldsymbol{\beta}_g^{\mathsf T}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{s,g}^{(\tau)},
    \qquad
    \min_{b_g,\boldsymbol{\beta}_g}
    \sum_{s\in\mathcal{T}}
    \left(z_{s,g}^{(\tau)}-\widehat{z}_{s,g}^{(\tau)}\right)^2
    +\alpha\|\boldsymbol{\beta}_g\|_2^2,
    \label{eq:ridge-objective}
\end{equation}
where $b_g$ is an unpenalized intercept, $\boldsymbol{\beta}_g$ contains the processed-feature coefficients, $\alpha$ controls shrinkage, and $\mathcal{T}$ is the training set. Using training data only, undefined numeric values are median-imputed, the four nonnegative influence features are log-compressed, near-constant numeric columns are disabled, and the remaining numeric columns are standardized; missing indicators are retained without standardization. The penalty is selected from $\{0.1,1,10,100\}$ by inner-validation SmoothL1 loss, after which preprocessing and Ridge are refitted on the complete outer-training partition and predictions are returned to nonnegative NV units.

\subsection{Subject-level evaluation}

M0 and compact Ridge use the same participants, targets, and ten subject-level outer folds. The outer-training partitions contained 130--133 participants and the held-out partitions contained 13--16; inner validation used only the corresponding outer-training participants. All transformations and model choices were training-only. The primary results below use pooled held-out $R^2$ and Spearman rank correlation. M0 was evaluated with three neural-network initialization settings and is reported as the mean and standard deviation across them. Ridge is deterministic for fixed folds, so only its single score is shown. This matched design supports a preliminary comparison but does not replace external validation.

\section{Preliminary results}

Table~\ref{tab:m0-ridge-results} summarizes the 18 reported measure--threshold endpoints. Updated compact Ridge had higher held-out $R^2$ than M0 for 17 endpoints and higher Spearman correlation for 16 endpoints. At threshold 0.05, both approaches predicted all six outcomes, while compact Ridge consistently produced the higher point estimates. At threshold 0.20, compact Ridge improved five of the six outcomes; average shortest path length was the exception for both $R^2$ and rank correlation.

At threshold 0.50, updated Ridge produced higher $R^2$ than M0 for all six outcomes and higher Spearman correlation for five. In the earlier ten-feature model, an extreme standardized finite-influence-fraction value for one held-out participant produced a path-length $R^2$ of $-3.154$. Removing this predictor while retaining the participant and the original folds increased path-length $R^2$ to 0.383 and Spearman correlation to 0.608. Small-worldness showed the converse comparison between evaluation measures: updated Ridge had higher $R^2$, whereas M0 had slightly higher Spearman correlation. Because the predictor removal was motivated by inspection of the earlier held-out error, the updated results are a follow-up ablation rather than independent evidence of improved generalization.

\section{Discussion and future work}

The feasibility experiment indicates that numerical sensitivity is partly encoded in a participant's standard connectome. M0 provides a full-information benchmark, whereas compact Ridge represents threshold proximity and the consequences of hypothetical edge changes using nine interpretable numeric features. The compact model's generally stronger point performance suggests that mechanism-informed summaries may be advantageous when the cohort is small relative to the dimensionality of the complete connectome.

Important limitations remain. Threshold proximity is not a calibrated MCA switching probability, and isolated edge toggles do not represent simultaneous or correlated changes. Influence calculation is limited to 200 candidates, path-based outcomes may be undefined on disconnected graphs, and the compact vector may omit predictive information contained in the full connectome or broader feature representations. Removing the validity fraction eliminated one observed extrapolation route but does not guarantee robustness to unusual values of the remaining features. Moreover, this change followed inspection of held-out predictions, so its apparent benefit may be optimistic. Findings from one study and processing configuration may also not generalize to other populations or acquisition conditions.

The next stage will treat M0 and the updated compact Ridge model as baselines. Planned work will test prespecified safeguards for extreme or unavailable influence features, evaluate feature and candidate-budget ablations, assess coefficient and prediction stability, and determine whether nonlinear models using the same compact representation improve performance. Evaluation will also distinguish prediction of NV magnitude from screening for unusually high numerical sensitivity. Most importantly, the nine-feature update requires confirmation in an untouched cohort or through a newly specified, fully nested validation analysis.
