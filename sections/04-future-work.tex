\chapter{Predicting Numerical Variability from Standard Functional Connectivity}
\label{chap:future-work}
\noindent\textbf{Abstract.}\enspace
Numerical perturbations in fMRI preprocessing can affect functional connectivity (FC) graph measures and contribute to false-positive or false-negative findings near decision thresholds. Assessing numerical variability (NV) through Monte Carlo Arithmetic (MCA) requires repeated processing and specialized instrumentation, limiting routine use.

This phase asks whether NV can be predicted for individual graph measures from one IEEE FC matrix, how much numerical sensitivity the measures share, and when a single participant-level score is sufficient. The pilot used MCA-derived targets for 146 participants with Parkinson's disease to compare BrainNetCNN (M0) on full signed FC with compact Ridge using nine IEEE-derived features. Ridge achieved higher held-out $R^2$ for 17 of 18 endpoints and higher Spearman correlation for 16.

\section{Introduction and research questions}

Phase~I demonstrated that NV in fMRI graph measures is measurable and can influence statistical conclusions. The cost of repeated MCA runs motivates the following research questions for Phase~II.

\begin{description}
  \item[RQ1a: Metric-specific prediction.] How accurately can the NV of each graph measure be predicted from a single IEEE FC matrix, and where does prediction fail?
  \item[RQ1b: Shared sensitivity.] To what extent do graph measures share a common pattern of numerical sensitivity, and how well can a single predicted participant-level score identify participants with high NV across measures?
  \item[\textcolor{red}{RQ2: Generalization.}] \textcolor{red}{Does a model trained on participants with PD generalize across populations and cohorts?}
  \item[\textcolor{red}{RQ3: Downstream use.}] \textcolor{red}{Can predicted NV recover cohort-level NPVR and significance-flip risk without MCA?}
\end{description}

Small numerical perturbations can change whether a connection close to the graph threshold is included in the binary graph. The effect of adding or removing an edge depends on its location in the network and on the graph measure. We therefore hypothesize that threshold proximity and the structural consequences of potential edge changes in the standard IEEE connectome can help predict MCA-derived NV.

Our pilot compares \textbf{M0}, a BrainNetCNN trained on the full, unthresholded signed FC matrix, and \textbf{M1}, a compact Ridge model using nine IEEE-derived features that summarize threshold proximity, density, and single-edge toggle responses. The proposed work will evaluate alternative features and architectures, failure modes across graph densities, and cross-cohort generalization.


\section{Data and methodology}

\subsection{Data, quality control, and prediction targets}

The pilot used the Parkinson's disease group from PPMI, with the RL acquisition and six-motion-parameter regression described in Chapter~\ref{chap:phase-one}. Minimal quality control excluded clear acquisition or processing failures, including scans with 92 or 13 of the expected 240 volumes, failed registration, and gross image defects such as large signal voids or spatial discontinuities. Volumes flagged as non-steady-state were removed before FC estimation. Of the 146 participants with a valid IEEE connectome, 140 retained ten MCA runs, five retained nine, and one retained eight.

We report the six binary graph measures from Phase~I at three thresholds ($\tau=0.05$, $0.20$, and $0.50$). For local measures, the target was the mean of the 100 regional standard deviations across MCA runs. This participant-level summary makes the pilot easier to evaluate. Regional prediction is an extension motivated by the spatial heterogeneity in Phase~I. For global measures, the target was the standard deviation across runs. At $\tau=0.50$, disconnected MCA graphs limited the evaluation of average shortest path length and small-worldness to 116 participants.

\subsection{Initial prediction models}

\paragraph{M0: Full-connectome baseline.}
M0 tests whether a model can learn numerical variability directly from the unthresholded, signed $100 \times 100$ IEEE FC matrix. Its BrainNetCNN encoder~\cite{Kawahara2017BrainNetCNN} uses edge-to-edge and edge-to-node layers followed by a dense layer and measure-specific outputs. The encoder jointly learns nine outputs, six of which are reported here for comparison with Phase~I. Each outer-fold prediction averages two refits of the selected configuration.

\paragraph{Auxiliary features.}
The nine features summarize threshold proximity, graph density, and the structural consequences of potential edge changes. For a given participant, an edge with IEEE correlation $C_e$ has threshold distance $d_e=\left|\,|C_e|-\tau\,\right|$. Among the $M=4{,}950$ possible undirected edges, we toggle each of the up to 200 closest edges within $d_e\leq 0.02$ in the IEEE graph. A toggle adds an absent edge or removes a present one. For a local measure, let $\delta_{e,i}$ be the resulting change at region $i$. The mean absolute and root-mean-square changes over the $N=100$ regions are
\begin{equation}
    a_e = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\delta_{e,i}|,
    \qquad
    r_e = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{e,i}^{\,2}}.
\end{equation}

For a global measure, $a_e=r_e=|\delta_e|$, where $\delta_e$ is the scalar change. In Table~\ref{tab:compact-ridge-features}, $\mathcal F$ is the set of toggles with finite responses and $K_{\mathrm{valid}}=|\mathcal F|$. The indicator $\mathbb I$ equals one when its condition holds, and $Q_p$ is the $p$-quantile across all possible edges.

\paragraph{M1: Compact Ridge regression.}
M1 tests whether these summaries suffice to predict NV. An $\ell_2$-regularized linear model was fitted separately for each measure and threshold, with imputation, missingness indicators, and feature standardization fitted on training data. Target scaling was inverted to report predictions on the original NV scale.

\subsection{Subject-level evaluation}

M0 and M1 used the same participants, targets, and ten subject-level outer folds at each threshold. The saved folds balanced composite NV ranks and kept each participant's repetitions together. Training-only inner validation selected the CNN configuration or Ridge penalty. Pooled held-out predictions were evaluated using $R^2$ and Spearman's $\rho$.

\section{Preliminary results}

At absolute thresholds of 0.05, 0.20, and 0.50, the 146 IEEE graphs retained an average of 96.9\%, 84.9\%, and 41.3\% of possible edges, respectively. The corresponding mean sparsities were 3.1\%, 15.1\%, and 58.7\%. At $\tau=0.50$, disconnected MCA graphs limited the evaluation of average shortest path length and small-worldness to 116 participants.

Ridge achieved higher held-out $R^2$ than M0 for 17 of 18 measure--threshold combinations and higher Spearman rank correlation ($\rho$) for 16 (Table~\ref{tab:m0-ridge-results}). At $\tau=0.05$, Ridge scored higher on both metrics for all six graph measures. At $\tau=0.20$, M0 scored higher on both only for average shortest path length. At $\tau=0.50$, Ridge had higher $R^2$ for all six measures, while M0 had slightly higher $\rho$ for small-worldness.

Future work will evaluate alternative features and models to improve prediction across measures and thresholds.

\section{Discussion and proposed work}

\paragraph{Pilot findings.}
In the pilot, nine IEEE-derived features predicted NV well for most endpoints (Ridge $R^2$ up to 0.84, Spearman $\rho$ up to 0.93), and a compact linear model outperformed a BrainNetCNN trained on the full connectome in 17 of 18 comparisons. This is consistent with the hypothesis that threshold proximity and edge-toggle influence carry much of the predictable signal. With only 146 participants, however, the comparison also favours low-capacity models. We will therefore extend the feature ablation to identify which of the nine features carry the signal, and evaluate richer models,that combines the learned IEEE-FC representation from the BrainNetCNN encoder with the nine auxiliary features.

\paragraph{Failure modes.}
Prediction was weakest for degree centrality at $\tau=0.50$ (Ridge $R^2=0.101$) and average shortest path length at $\tau=0.20$ ($R^2=0.186$), although rank correlations remained positive ($\rho=0.500$ and $0.686$). Preserved ranking with poor calibration limits use for RQ3, because NPVR depends on the magnitude of NV and not only on its ordering. We consider three candidate explanations: (i) few edges lie near the threshold in these graphs, leaving little signal for proximity features; (ii) the target is skewed or has low variance; and (iii) path-length changes depend on global, nonlinear effects that single-edge toggles do not capture. For average shortest path length at $\tau=0.20$ the BrainNetCNN scored slightly higher $R^2$ than Ridge (0.222 vs.\ 0.186), which motivates a hybrid model that combines a learned IEEE-FC representation with the nine auxiliary features.

\paragraph{Metric-specific versus participant-level prediction.}
Metric-specific predictions retain each measure's NV in its original units and show which measures are variable. A participant-level score (the mean or maximum of standardized NV across measures) simplifies screening but can hide which measure drives it. In preliminary pair plots of observed MCA-derived NV, median pairwise $R^2$ across the six reported measures was 0.80, 0.77, and 0.57 at $\tau=0.05$, $0.20$, and $0.50$, respectively. At $\tau=0.50$, degree centrality NV had weak associations with the other measures (pairwise $R^2\leq 0.16$). We will define high-NV participants [e.g., top quartile], compare screening performance across thresholds, and identify cases where a summary score misses measure-specific variability.

\paragraph{Validation.}
We will develop and select models using the 146 PPMI participants with Parkinson's disease, then fix them and evaluate transfer in the 38 PPMI healthy controls using their existing MCA outputs as targets. This tests population transfer under the same scanner protocol and pipeline. For external validation we will use resting-state fMRI from the NKI-Rockland Sample~\cite{Nooner2012NKI}, which differs in cohort and acquisition, and generate compatible IEEE and MCA outputs.

\paragraph{Downstream use.}
Finally, we will test whether predicted NV supports estimates of NPVR, $p$-value uncertainty ($\sigma_p$), and significance-flip probability ($P_{\mathrm{flip}}$) without repeated MCA. Because NPVR is defined from per-participant variance, this will require predicting per-participant variance (or log-SD) and not only the mean of regional SDs; $\sigma_{\mathrm{pop}}$ can be computed directly from the IEEE outputs. We will evaluate agreement with MCA-based NPVR on bootstrapped cohorts of different sizes and assess whether the fragile versus robust classification of results is preserved. Any predictor will need re-validation for pipelines other than the one studied here.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The pilot uses one cohort, one pipeline, and one atlas; 146 participants; and a participant-level target averaged over regions. Regional prediction, other denoising strategies, and proportional thresholding are natural extensions but are outside the pilot.
